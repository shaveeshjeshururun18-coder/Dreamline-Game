Why Simple 3D Waves Look Fake x' = x - (Q/k) D·A sin(θ)

Basic 3D scenes only displace height y = A cos(kx - ωt). In real oceans, water molecules move in orbital circles—moving forward at the crest and backward in the trough. Our vertex shader shifts vertices horizontally toward the crest, creating sharp, pinched peaks and broad, flat troughs (Trochoidal / Gerstner waves).

Dispersion Relation & Wave Cascades ω(k) = √(g · k)

Long swells travel faster than short chop because phase speed depends on wavelength: c = √(gλ / 2π). We sum 8 directional Gerstner swells in the vertex shader plus 10 octaves of animated capillary wind ripples in the fragment shader so waves never repeat mechanically.

Coastal Shoaling, Swash & Excavation Physics c_s = √(g · h)

As deep-water waves enter shallow coastal bathymetry (h < λ/2), bottom friction compresses wavelength and increases steepness until crests pitch forward into turbulent surf foam. Digging trenches or building sandbars on the shore dynamically alters water depth h(x,z), redirecting wave shoaling, caustics, and tidal swash in real time.
